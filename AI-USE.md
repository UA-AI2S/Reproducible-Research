In reproducible research, disclosing AI use is critical because other researchers need to know exactly how data was analyzed or how scripts were generated to replicate your workflow. If AI assisted with substantive tasks like writing code, processing data, or screening literature, it must be disclosed. [1, 2, 3] 
Depending on where the AI was used, disclosures are typically formatted into the Methods section (for data/code tracking) or the Acknowledgments (for writing assistance). [4, 5] 
## 1. Code Generation Disclosure (Placed in Methods/Appendix)
If you used an LLM to generate programming scripts for data cleaning, visualization, or statistical models, you must provide enough technical context for replication. [4, 6] 

“Analysis scripts for the baseline logistic regression models were generated using Anthropic Claude 3.5 Sonnet (accessed June 2025). The specific system prompt used was: [Insert exact markdown prompt here or link to repository Appendix]. All AI-generated Python code was manually reviewed, debugged, and validated by the authors to confirm statistical precision and the exact reproducibility of the results.” [4, 6] 

## 2. Data Processing & Methodology Disclosure (Placed in Methods)
If AI assisted in organizing data, extracting text, or identifying categorical qualitative themes. [4] 

“Qualitative literature screening and text classification were assisted by OpenAI's GPT-4 (v4-2024-04-09). The tool was utilized to screen 542 abstracts based on our predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. All AI-sorted classifications were systematically cross-checked and validated by two human reviewers, who made the final inclusion decisions. The full API request log and parameters are available in the project’s GitHub repository.” [2, 4, 6] 

## 3. Writing and Structural Assistance (Placed in Acknowledgments)
Basic spelling and grammar checkers do not require disclosure, but using generative AI to draft summaries, refine logic flow, or translate sections does. [2, 7] 

“The authors used OpenAI's ChatGPT (Jan 2026 version) to draft initial structural outlines and refine the clarity of the narrative text in Section 3. All generated content was thoroughly reviewed, rewritten, and verified for factual accuracy by the authors, who retain full accountability for the integrity of the published manuscript.” [2, 5, 6, 8]

## 📋 The 5 Core Elements of an AI Disclosure
No matter your wording, major publishers (like [Wiley](https://www.wiley.com/) and [Elsevier](https://www.elsevier.com/)) look for these 5 pieces of information: [2] 

   1. Specific Tool Name & Developer: (e.g., ChatGPT by OpenAI, Claude by Anthropic)
   2. Version or Date of Access: Because models shift rapidly over time.
   3. The Exact Task: What did it do? (e.g., generating R scripts, clustering text)
   4. Human Verification Statement: Explicitly states that humans double-checked the work for errors or "hallucinations".
   5. Human Accountability: Confirming that AI is not credited as a co-author and that the human authors take full legal responsibility for the data. [2, 3, 5, 6, 9, 10]
